Thanks for testing Cloudy.

Cloudy plays your own music and films from a phone in your house, on every other phone you own. No account, no subscription, and nothing uploaded anywhere. This page gets you set up in about ten minutes and tells you what we'd like you to try.

Cloudy Server

Goes on one Android phone or tablet that stays at home, plugged in. It holds the music and films and serves them to everyone else. An old phone is perfect.

Cloudy

Goes on every phone you'll listen and watch on. One install gives you two icons: Cloudy Music and Cloudy Theater.

Before you start

You'll get two links, one for each app. Open each on your phone, signed in to the Google account you gave us, tap Become a tester, then follow the link to Google Play and install. It can take a few minutes after joining before Play lets you install.

You need Android 8 or newer. Everything happens on your home Wi-Fi, so keep the phones on the same network.

Set up the server

One person per household does this, on the phone that will stay home.

  1. Put some music or films on that phone.

    Anything already in its Music, Movies or Download folders counts. Your own files only, please.

  2. Open Cloudy Server and tap Set it up.

    Allow access to music, videos and photos when asked. Photos is only for posters kept beside films. Allow notifications too: that's how you'll see people asking to join.

  3. Let it look through your files.

    It starts serving on its own when it's done. The big button on the main screen shows whether it's running, and a notification shows its address.

  4. Tap Allow background on the main screen.

    Otherwise your phone's battery saver may put the server to sleep. Leave the phone plugged in and it can live in a drawer.

Join from your other phones

  1. Open Cloudy and tap Skip in the corner

    or step through the short introduction. You'll land on Connect to your library, which finds servers on your Wi-Fi by itself.

  2. Tap Ask to join next to your server.

    Your phone shows a four-digit number and waits.

  3. Approve it on the server phone.

    A notification appears there saying your phone wants to join, with Approve and Deny. Approve only if it shows the same number.

  4. Pick who you are.

    Cloudy asks Who's listening? Tap your name, add yourself with Someone else, or come in as Guest. Each person gets their own likes, playlists and place in every film.

The numbers are how you know it's your phone asking and not somebody else on the Wi-Fi. Joining this way only works on the same Wi-Fi as the server. Anyone can still use a code instead: on the server, open Settings and tap Show a pairing code, then Scan QR code in Cloudy.

Whoever set up the server should pick Me on their own phone. That's the admin profile: it can approve new phones, remove them, and change the server. Rename it to your name from Settings in Cloudy. Once you're the admin, requests from new phones also pop up on your own phone while Cloudy is open.

What to try

Tick things off as you go. The ticks stay on this device only.

Telling us what went wrong

CloudyServerDev@gmail.com

Anything confusing counts, not only crashes. A good report says:

  • which phone, and which app (Server, Music or Theater)
  • what you tapped just before it went wrong
  • a screenshot, if you can

If Cloudy Server crashes, it shows a card on its main screen next time it opens, with a button to send the details. It never sends anything unless you press it.

Good to know

Away from home

Cloudy works on your home Wi-Fi out of the box. Reaching your library from outside is optional and needs more setup; stick to home for this test unless you're curious.

Join requests need an open app

The server phone always shows them. An admin's own phone only shows them while Cloudy is open or playing.

Android only

There's no iPhone app yet.

Updates arrive through Play

We'll ship fixes during the test. Keep auto-update on, or check Play now and then.

Please stay in for 14 days

Google needs 12 testers to stay opted in for 14 days in a row before Cloudy can go public. Leaving the test, or uninstalling, resets that count, so please keep both apps installed even if you only open them now and then.
